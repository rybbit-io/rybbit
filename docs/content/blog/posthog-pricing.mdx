PostHog pricing is usage-based and billed separately for each product. Every product has a free allowance that resets monthly (1 million analytics events, 5,000 session recordings, 1 million feature flag requests, 100,000 exceptions and 1,500 survey responses, among others), and you don't need a card to use it. Past the allowance you pay per unit on a sliding scale: analytics events start at $0.00005 each ($50 per million) and fall to $0.000009, web recordings start at $0.005 and fall to $0.0015, and flag requests start at $0.0001. There are no seats, minimums or contracts, and you can cap each product with a billing limit. Two things push real bills above the headline rate: events from identified users carry an extra person-profile charge, and each product you switch on is metered on its own.

The free tier is genuinely generous. PostHog's [official pricing page](https://posthog.com/pricing) says 97% of companies using it pay nothing, and a small SaaS can run analytics, replay, feature flags and error tracking without a bill (see worked example 1).

Disclosure: we build [Rybbit](/features/web-analytics), an open-source analytics tool that appears in the comparison near the end; [Rybbit vs PostHog](/compare/posthog) covers features. Every PostHog number here comes from posthog.com (the pricing page and its calculator, the per-product pricing pages, PostHog's docs and its public handbook), linked inline and checked in September 2026. Prices are as of September 2026, in US dollars, excluding tax.

## How PostHog's usage-based pricing works

- **Graduated tiers.** Each band's rate applies only to the units inside it. Five million events cost $0 for the first million, $50 for the second and $102.90 for the next three ($0.0000343 each), not the lowest rate on all five million.
- **Allowances reset monthly** and stay the same whether or not you have a card on file. Unused allowance doesn't roll over.
- **You pay on ingestion only.** PostHog charges when data is first received and has no storage fees.
- **No seats, minimums or commitments.** Billing is monthly on your sign-up date, the first charge comes a month after sign-up, and cards are charged through Stripe. Paying by invoice requires buying credits up front.

## Free plan vs pay-as-you-go

Both self-serve plans get the same free allowances. The difference is what happens past them.

| | Free | Pay-as-you-go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | $0, no card | $0 until you pass an allowance, then per unit |
| Projects | 1 | 6 |
| Event data retention | 1 year | 7 years |
| Recording retention | 30 days | 90 days |
| Team members | Unlimited | No per-seat charges |
| Support | Community | Email (Slack above $2,000/month) |
| Past the allowance | Extra events are dropped; flags return a quota-limited response | Billed per unit, up to any billing limit you set |

PostHog recommends pay-as-you-go for "nearly everyone": it unlocks all features and still costs nothing within the allowances, which a billing limit can enforce.

## Product analytics pricing: events

Product and web analytics share one meter: PostHog bills "events captured – pageviews, clicks, custom events, and everything autocapture picks up" ([product analytics pricing](https://posthog.com/product-analytics/pricing)), and [web analytics](https://posthog.com/web-analytics/pricing) uses the same events. Heatmaps don't add to the count: PostHog's [autocapture docs](https://posthog.com/docs/product-analytics/autocapture) say heatmap capture creates no additional events.

### Identified vs anonymous events

Every event pays the base rate. Events from identified users (or users with custom person properties) also pay a **person profiles** rate, with its own tiers and its own first million free. From the breakdown in PostHog's calculator:

| Monthly volume | Base rate (all events) | Person profiles (identified events only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 1M | Free | Free |
| 1M–2M | $0.0000500 | $0.0001980 |
| 2M–15M | $0.0000343 | $0.0000697 |
| 15M–50M | $0.0000295 | $0.0000360 |
| 50M–100M | $0.0000218 | $0.0000146 |
| 100M–250M | $0.0000150 | $0.0000037 |
| 250M+ | $0.0000090 | $0.0000010 |

Base tiers count all events; person-profile tiers count identified events only. In the 1M–2M band an identified event costs $0.000248 against $0.00005 for an anonymous one, about five times as much; from 2M to 15M it's about three times, and above 100M only 1.1 to 1.25 times. PostHog's own pages round this differently ("up to 4x" in the docs, "10x" on the web analytics page); the rates above are the ones its calculator uses.

The JavaScript SDK defaults to `person_profiles: 'identified_only'`, so visitors stay anonymous until you call `identify()`, after which all of that user's events are identified. Backend SDKs and the API capture identified events by default ([anonymous vs identified events](https://posthog.com/docs/data/anonymous-vs-identified-events)). A marketing site with no logins can run entirely on the base rate; a logged-in SaaS app mostly can't.

**Group analytics**, a paid add-on for account-level B2B analysis, is billed on every identified event in the project once you subscribe: first 1M free, then $0.000071 per event, falling to $0.0000029 above 250M ([group analytics pricing](https://posthog.com/group-analytics/pricing)).

## Feature flags and experiments pricing: requests

| Flag requests per month | Price per request |
| --- | --- |
| First 1M | Free |
| 1M–2M | $0.000100 |
| 2M–10M | $0.000045 |
| 10M–50M | $0.000025 |
| 50M+ | $0.000010 |

A request is a call to PostHog's `/flags` endpoint, not each flag check in your code ([feature flags pricing](https://posthog.com/feature-flags/pricing)). In the browser, the SDK makes one when it initializes, when a user is identified or their properties change, when you reload flags and when you create a group. On a server, each evaluation call is a request unless you use local evaluation, where every poll for flag definitions counts as 10 requests; at the default 30-second interval that's 864,000 requests a month per server ([estimating usage and costs](https://posthog.com/docs/billing/estimating-usage-costs)). Surveys evaluate flags for targeting, so they generate requests too.

## Billing limits, alerts and what happens when you go over

On pay-as-you-go, usage past the allowance is billed until you cap it. The controls, per PostHog's [billing limits](https://posthog.com/docs/billing/limits-alerts) and [spike detection](https://posthog.com/docs/billing/spike-detection) docs:

- **Billing limits per product.** At the limit PostHog stops ingesting that product's data, and the extra data is lost for good. Feature flags return a `quota_limited` response, and SDKs fall back to default values.
- **Alert emails** go to organization owners at 80% and 100% of each limit and of each free allowance.
- **Spike detection** runs daily and emails self-serve customers about unusual jumps or drops, at most once a week.
- **A billable-usage dashboard template** breaks usage down by event, SDK and product.

A hard limit means a traffic spike leaves a gap in your data until the billing period resets or you raise the limit, so set limits with headroom. PostHog's pricing page also says that if you get an unexpected bill, "we'll pretty much always refund it".

## Discounts: startups, non-profits and prepaid credits

- **Startups:** $50,000 in credits for 12 months if your company is under 2 years old, has raised less than $5 million, signed up on or after January 1, 2023, and applies from a company email domain. Since September 14, 2026, credits no longer cover PostHog's AI tools (PostHog Desktop, the Slack app, Replay Vision, PostHog AI and Inbox). Y Combinator companies get a separate deal ([startup program](https://posthog.com/startups)).
- **Non-profits:** the pricing FAQ says there's a discount "in most cases" once you contact sales. PostHog's public sales handbook sets it at 15% on credit purchases under $25,000 and an extra 5% on purchases of $25,000–$100,000 ([contract rules](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/contract-rules)).
- **Prepaid credits:** monthly pay-as-you-go gets no discount beyond the built-in volume tiers. Buying credits up front, usually for 12 months, does: 20% off at $25,000–$59,000 a year, 25% at $60,000–$99,000, 30% at $100,000–$249,000, 35% at $250,000–$499,000 and 40% at $500,000–$999,000. Self-serve accounts with at least three paid invoices averaging $280 or more can buy credits in the app at 10% off. Half of any unused credit rolls over if you renew at equal or higher spend ([pre-paid plans](https://posthog.com/docs/billing/pre-paid-plans)).

## How to keep your PostHog bill down

From PostHog's own cost-cutting docs:

1. **Keep events anonymous unless you need person data.** Leave the JavaScript SDK on `person_profiles: 'identified_only'`, and call `identify()` and `group()` once per session. On backend SDKs, set `$process_person_profile` to `false` for events that don't need a profile ([product analytics costs](https://posthog.com/docs/product-analytics/cutting-costs)).
2. **Trim autocapture** with an allow or ignore list, or turn it off and send custom events for what you actually analyze.
3. **Drop automatic events you don't use.** You can disable automatic `$pageview` and `$pageleave` capture and send them manually where they matter; bounce rate may break.
4. **Record fewer sessions.** Replay trigger groups let you sample a percentage of sessions, start recording on a URL or event, gate recording behind a feature flag, and set a minimum duration so quick bounces aren't recorded ([recording controls](https://posthog.com/docs/session-replay/how-to-control-which-sessions-you-record)).
5. **Cut flag requests.** Skip the first-load request (`advanced_disable_feature_flags_on_first_load`), bootstrap flags, poll for local evaluation every 5 minutes instead of 30 seconds, avoid local evaluation in edge or Lambda functions, disable flags in test and CI, and find forgotten environments still calling `/flags` ([feature flag costs](https://posthog.com/docs/feature-flags/cutting-costs)).
6. **Set a billing limit on every product** and build the billable-usage dashboard from PostHog's template to see which events and SDKs drive the bill.
7. **Prepay once spend is stable.** Bills above about $2,100 a month cross the $25,000-a-year prepaid threshold; smaller accounts can use the 10% self-serve credit purchase.

## PostHog vs alternatives at the same volume

Per-unit prices only compare cleanly if the units match, and events don't.

- **PostHog** bills every event its SDKs capture. By default the JavaScript SDK sends a `$pageview` and a `$pageleave` for each page, plus autocapture events for clicks, form changes and copies. Identified events pay the person-profile rate on top.
- **Rybbit** bills by events too, but counts fewer of them: pageviews, custom events and outbound-link clicks by default, plus Web Vitals, button clicks, copies and form interactions only if you switch those on. Error events, session replays and identify calls don't count, and logged-in users cost the same as anonymous ones. ## FAQ

**How much does PostHog cost per month?**
Nothing until you pass a product's free allowance. After that it depends on volume and mix: 2 million anonymous events cost $50 a month, and 5 million events (2 million identified) plus 50,000 recordings and 2 million flag requests cost $623.40. The [calculator](https://posthog.com/pricing) on PostHog's pricing page estimates your own mix.

**What does the PostHog free tier include in 2026?**
Every month: 1M analytics events, 5,000 web and 2,500 mobile recordings, 1M flag requests, 100,000 exceptions, 1,500 survey responses, 1M warehouse rows, 100,000 AI observability events, 10 GB of logs, plus pipeline, workflow and AI-credit allowances. The free plan adds 1 project, 1-year data retention, unlimited team members and community support. It doesn't expire, and no card is needed.

**Does PostHog's free allowance reset every month?**
Yes. Allowances reset monthly, stay the same after you add a card, and don't roll over.

**Why do identified events cost more?**
PostHog creates a person profile for identified users, and querying their events needs more expensive table joins. So identified events pay a person-profile rate on top of the base rate: $0.000198 extra per event between 1M and 2M, falling to $0.000001 above 250M, with the first 1M identified events exempt.

**Can I cap my PostHog bill?**
Yes, with a billing limit on each product. At the limit PostHog stops ingesting that product's data (it's dropped, not queued) and feature flags return default values.

**Does PostHog offer startup or non-profit discounts?**
Yes. Startups under 2 years old that have raised less than $5 million can get $50,000 in credits for 12 months. Non-profits get a discount through sales; PostHog's handbook puts it at 15% on credit purchases under $25,000.

**Is PostHog cheaper than FullStory?**
FullStory doesn't publish paid prices. Its [plans page](https://www.fullstory.com/plans/) lists a free plan with 30,000 sessions a month, 12 months of data retention and up to 10 users, and quotes everything above that. On PostHog, the first 5,000 recordings each month are free and 30,000 recordings cost $102.50 on pay-as-you-go (10,000 × $0.005 plus 15,000 × $0.0035). For other replay options, see our [FullStory alternatives](/blog/fullstory-alternatives) guide.
